Kotkin’s main point is that the Soviet Union collapsed not because of heroic revolutionaries or organized civil society - rather it was the Moscow government intentionally forfeiting its crackdown backstop in order to encourage reform at the periphery.

> The 1989 revolutions did not happen because of a broad freedom drive or an establishment self-enrichment grab. The cave-in was unintended, precipitated by Gorbachev’s unilateral removal of the Soviet backstop, a move that had been intended to goad socialist-bloc countries to reform themselves. In other words, Gorbachev was looking to galvanize the reform-minded Gorbachevs of Eastern Europe. There was only one flaw in this approach: there were no East European Gorbachevs.

> in 1989 “civil society” could not have shattered Soviet-style socialism for the simple reason that civil society in Eastern Europe did not then actually exist. The mostly small groups of dissidents, however important morally, could not have constituted any kind of society. On the contrary, it was the establishment—the “uncivil society”—that brought down its own system.

The foreign policy considerations of the Soviet Union seem quite different from China today. Despite both of them having a Leninist system. It does not seem important to China that its allies share its system of government. But the 1956 crackdown on Hungary and the 1968 crackdown on Czechoslovakia were motivated because the Soviets felt that if any country in the Warsaw Pact did go capitalist or democratic, it would actually discredit their whole regime.
